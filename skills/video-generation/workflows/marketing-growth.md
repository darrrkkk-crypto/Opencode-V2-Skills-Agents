# Marketing and growth shorts

Use for performance ads, e-commerce, promotions, and conversion-oriented social videos. Stop the scroll in the first two seconds with clear information, visible movement, and synchronized sound, explain the product's core value, build trust with facts, then give one clear action. Reuse the known product, audience, evidence, and objective; when revising a line or swapping an asset in an existing ad, address only the corresponding beat

## Four-beat structure

15–30 seconds is a common finished length. The table below offers an adjustable timing allocation. Arrange each beat according to the actual amount of information and the length of the evidence; use shot numbers and written rhythm when calling a video model

| Beat | Timing reference | Creative task and image | Rhythm and sound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0–2s | Show the strongest result, contrast, or action immediately so viewers understand the point at a glance | Begin with subject action or camera movement and synchronized sound from frame 0; the title emphasizes the same point |
| Detail | 2–10s | Product hero visual, unboxing, first use, or key feature in motion | Keep introducing new details while allowing time to understand the operation |
| Proof | 10–22s | Measurable before/after comparison, demonstration result, or real testimonial | Compress the process and give viewing time to the result |
| CTA | Last 2–3s | A logo, offer card, arrow, or benefit points toward a clear action | Large action text; music resolves and fades out |

## Five advertising archetypes

| Archetype | Common length | Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct response | 15–25s | Hard cuts and a direct hook; specify time, money, or labor saved, or anxiety reduced |
| E-commerce SKU | 10–18s | Alternate material and craft macros with actual use; reduce spoken delivery and lead with product images, dense visual stimulation, and brief on-screen text |
| Creator talking head + B-roll | 15–30s | Selfie footage, first-person experience, or recommendations, intercut with 2–4-second product and usage details |
| Problem versus solution | 20–30s | Compare the same task: the old approach is cool, flat, and slow; the new approach is warm, bright, and easy |
| Local business | 15–25s | About 1 second on the storefront, about 2 seconds on a signature item, then a satisfied customer and a specific offer |

Choose one main line of expression for the main film. Edit existing assets when they are sufficient; read the corresponding execution guidance only when images, video, or sound are actually missing. Separately delivered model clips can be generated directly; use local file-based assembly for subtitles, music, or multi-asset arrangement

## Copy patterns

Choose a hook pattern from the actual problem or result in this task; the examples demonstrate sentence forms:

- Counterintuitive: “Why is your report still unfinished after three hours of overtime?”
- Warning: “Don't buy this unless you really want to stop staying up late.”
- Pain point: “If this happens to you too, save this method now.”
- Result first: “Ten seconds. The whole edit is done. Watch.”

End with one executable next step, using confirmed information for benefits and conditions:

- Start free: “Click the link below to start for free.”
- Limited-time offer: “Today only: save fifty. Click through to buy.”
- Get content: “Follow for the complete tool list.”
- Stock reminder: “Limited stock. First come, first served.”

## Shot language

Catch the Hook with a close-up, explain with a medium shot, provide product evidence through a macro, and finish the CTA with a wide shot or close-up. Changes in shot size clarify information levels and prevent 20 seconds of the same kind of image from becoming monotonous

Choose [shot density](../capabilities/model-video-generation.md#shot-density) according to the platform and content, retaining enough time to understand complete operations and evidence. Describe camera movement in every shot: prefer pushing in to reveal details, tracking to show use, and pulling back to reveal before-and-after contrasts, coordinated with subject action and sound. Push the background back so the product, text, and key actions become the visual center

For a complete model-generated ad, follow the guidance to [organize multiple shots in one request](../capabilities/model-video-generation.md#default-plan-the-complete-multi-shot-video). Do not send four separate requests just because the ad has Hook, Detail, Proof, and CTA beats

Use the strongest image or most powerful line in the first frame to bring viewers into the content immediately. Show key selling points, prices, offers, and CTAs in large type with clear contrast; platform controls usually occupy the bottom fifth of a 9:16 frame, so place key information higher and check the specific platform's safe area

## Sound coordination

When voice-over is needed, use clear articulation with authentic, energetic delivery and tighten meaningless gaps. E-commerce SKU videos can reduce spoken delivery and let product images convey value. Use modern, forward-moving instrumental music and keep it consistently below speech when voices are present

Align SFX with specific events: a click emphasizes a pain point or action, a whoosh connects a transition, and a chime lands with a number, button, or result. Let sound help viewers notice the evidence, and give the result enough viewing time

## Completion checks

- Information, movement, and sound together form an effective hook in the first two seconds, and the first frame enters the content immediately
- Shot changes serve information, while complete actions and evidence get enough comprehension time
- Product appearance, actual use, and core value are clear, with evidence for promises
- The CTA is a clear action; offers, numbers, and feedback all come from confirmed information
- Musical beats coordinate with images, voices are clear, and key text stays within the platform's safe area
